## Opening Discussion — On Learning Palmistry

> [!QUOTE] Teacher's opening remark
> A student was told her photo had been shared in the group; her Sun mount was noted to be roughly in the centre of the palm, with a slight shift toward Mercury.

- [!WARNING] Common mistake — A student cannot expect to read her **own** hand accurately this early. Self-prediction requires that the rekhāen (lines) and chinh (signs/marks) first be learned properly — **"itni jaldi nahi kar paoge"** (you won't be able to do it this quickly).
- **Palmistry (Hastarekha Śāstra) is described as a very slow subject.**
- [!SUCCESS] Basic requirement before any prediction: understanding the **quality of the hand** itself (e.g., fire-hand/nature) — this must be learned before attempting career, marriage, etc. predictions.

### On leaders/ministers and the hand
- **Q:** Do people who become ministers have something distinct in their hand — some separate "science"?
- **A:** At a high level one looks at:
  1. Sun mount
  2. Sun's rekhāen (lines)
  3. Bhāgya rekhā (Fate/Fortune Line)
  4. Jīvan rekhā (Life Line)
  5. All lines together

> [!IMPORTANT] Core reading principle
> **No single line gives a prediction by itself.** A combined/mixed impact of all indicators must be read together. One rekhā alone cannot be used to declare an outcome.

### Case Study — The Beggar Woman's Hand
> [!EXAMPLE] Case: elderly beggar woman, hand examined in passing (while the teacher was at a registry office)
> - Her **bhāgya rekhā** (Fate Line) ran complete and strong from start to end.
> - Yet her life was one of struggle (begging).
> - **Interpretation given:** the strong, complete fate line showed she was *working* until the very end (old age) — she was still active/engaged, not idle, even while begging.
> - Her **Budh (Mercury) finger** (little finger) was also good — reflected in her manner of speaking. When someone told her "we won't give, we don't have," she retorted: *"Khaali haath aa gaye kya? Hai kaise nahi, paisa toh hai jeb mein"* ("Did you come empty-handed? How can there not be — there is money in the pocket").
> - **Teaching point:** Her *way of responding* (sharp, confident) reflected her Mercury finger quality — showing that a good line/mount can manifest even within an outwardly poor material circumstance.

> [!NOTE] General teaching
> You must learn, gradually, **to read/recognize people** — this comes with practice, not instantly.

### On Learning Timeline
- [!TIP] Just as astrology (Jyotiṣa) has roughly a 6-month course, **palmistry requires about 1 to 1.5 years of practice — even after the initial learning/course is complete** — to become capable of prediction from lines.
- **Speaker emphasized repeatedly:** Palmistry is a *very* slow subject ("bahut hi slow subject hai") — the more hands one examines, the more practice/skill builds.

## Sun Mount — Determining Its Condition

> [!INFO] Definition
> The **Sun mount** (also called Ravi/Sūrya Parvat) sits below the ring finger (Sun finger/Anamikā).

### Comparison method with Jupiter and Saturn mounts
- With **Jupiter** and **Saturn** mounts, the earlier teaching was: the mount should have a slight *dhalwa* (sloped/inclined) quality to be considered good/normal.
- **Sun mount condition differs:** For Sun mount, the *correct* condition for "normal/sāmānya" is:
  - The mount should be **uthā huā** (raised) and **phulā huā** (puffed up) — **not spongy**.
  - This raised, puffed mount = **normal (sāmānya) condition**.

### Three Degrees of Sun Mount Development

| Condition | Description |
|---|---|
| **Sāmānya (Normal/Balanced)** | Mount is raised and puffed, not spongy |
| **Atidikshit (Over-developed)** | Mount is raised to an excessive degree — as if a separate lump of flesh/mass has been placed there; Sun's qualities become excessive |
| **Nimna (Under-developed/Depressed)** | Mount is flat/absent — Sun mount is barely visible on the palm |

## The Three Elements Used for All Sun-Related Predictions

> [!IMPORTANT] Whenever giving any Sun-related prediction, three things must be examined together:
> 1. **Sun finger** (Anamikā / ring finger)
> 2. **Sun mount**
> 3. **Sun line**

### Sun Line — Position and Timing
- The Sun line is generally seen in the palm **starting above the hṛdaya rekhā** (Heart Line).
- **Very few hands** show the Sun line starting at an initial/lower level — i.e., originating from the Moon mount, from the Fate Line, or from the Venus mount (Shukra Parvat). This is rare.
- **Typical/common pattern:** Sun line begins above the Heart Line, i.e., it becomes visible around **age 45–50**.
- [!NOTE] Interpretive consequence: **Fame (prasiddhi)** connected to the Sun line, in most hands, is likely to arrive around **age 50–55**.
  - Example given: when children grow up and succeed (e.g., get admission somewhere), people say "Oh, that's their son who did so well" — the fame is attributed to the parents. This illustrates why fame from the Sun line tends to appear later in life (in the parents' timeline), consistent with the line's late appearance above the Heart Line.

### Sun Finger — Function
> [!INFO] Definition — the finger as "receiver"
> The **finger is the receiver of energy** — it receives energy that comes "from the sky above."

> [!INFO] Definition — the mount as "storage"
> The **mount is the storage** of the energy received. Energy is received via the finger and stored in the mount.

### Combinations of Finger and Mount Quality

| Finger | Mount | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Weak/short (poor receiver) | Good mount | Less energy received, but what is received is well-stored — office/storage is good |
| Long (good receiver) | Poor mount | Reception is strong, but storage capacity is deficient |
| Both finger **and** mount good | — | Very good condition overall |
| Finger somewhat weak/thin/short **but** mount good **and** the line on it good | — | Even with one factor weak, if mount and line are fine, results will still be **60–70% accurate/positive** regarding Sun matters |

> [!CAUTION] Additional factor — dhairya (patience/steadiness)
> If a person lacks **dhairya** (patience/endurance), the Sun's positive qualities get disturbed, even with good mount/line/finger. Saturn's role — and in fact **all planets'** roles — come into play here as well.

> [!TIP] General learning approach
> When learning any subject, proceed slowly ("aaram aaram se") — **no rushing** with any topic. Eventually, prediction ability will develop.

## Sun Mount — Normal/Balanced (Sāmānya) Condition: Full List of Qualities

### 1. Self-Satisfaction (Ātma-santuṣṭi)
- This is the **first quality** associated with a balanced Sun.
- Meaning: regardless of whether success is achieved or not, there is an inner feeling of **contentment that effort was made** ("chalo maine koshish toh ki").
- Two possible attitudes toward one's own effort:
  1. **Negative framing:** "I didn't really do this" — dismissive of one's own effort (undesirable pattern).
  2. **Positive framing:** "I tried; yes it was hard, but see how far I came — I at least learned something new." — This **positive attitude gives both Jupiter's blessing and Sun mount's quality**, and is the source of true satisfaction.
- [!SUCCESS] When this positivity is present, patience (dhairya) naturally follows, and satisfaction arises automatically.
- **Study method suggested by teacher:** Compare such hands with a person whose **Saturn** is strong in the kuṇḍalī (birth chart) — observing Saturn-dominant hands helps sharpen palmistry perception, because familiarity with astrology (learned earlier) makes it easier to connect chart indications to hand indications. Reading the kuṇḍalī first, then the hand, accelerates understanding (vs. learning hand-reading before chart-reading, which would be harder).

### 2. Name & Fame (Nām-Fame)
- A person with balanced Sun mount generally receives good name-fame.
- Whether or not fame comes, such a person will always show **respect (ādar)** — even a small compliment is accepted graciously.
- After receiving a big compliment, there is **no ego/arrogance** ("ghamand nahi").
- Balanced reaction to both extremes:
  - Excess praise → does not become inflated with pride.
  - Little or no praise → does not complain or fish for more compliments.
- [!SUCCESS] Such people often receive fame **without seeking it** — fame comes to them on its own, through others' spontaneous praise/respect — as opposed to needing to spend money campaigning for recognition (example given: politicians organizing rallies, posters, self-promotion to gain fame, vs. someone people naturally praise as soon as they arrive).

### 3. Education & the Vidyā Rekhā (Line of Knowledge)
- Education is also read via **Budh (Mercury)**.
- **On the Sun mount itself there can be a line** — the teacher demonstrates on her own hand.
- **Location:** Between the Sun finger (ring finger) and the Saturn finger (middle finger), in the gap between the two fingers.
- **Direction distinguishes two different lines:**
  - A line running **from below upward** = **Sun line**.
  - A line running **from above downward**, in the same gap = **Vidyā rekhā** ("Line of Knowledge").
- The Vidyā rekhā can also appear on the **Jupiter mount** side (from the other direction, between Jupiter and Saturn fingers) — also called Vidyā rekhā there.
- **Quantity varies by hand:** some hands show one such line, some two, some three (a student reported three lines; another reported a single line that appeared "doubled/copied").
- [!NOTE] Clarification during class: A line running from **below the Sun line, joining downward**, is a **second Sun line**, not automatically a Vidyā rekhā — needs to be checked directly on the hand to determine which it is.

> [!INFO] Meaning of "Vidyā" here
> Vidyā (knowledge) here does **not** mean formal degrees collected. It means: **the ability/inclination to learn something from every situation and every person** — an eagerness to learn from everything.

> [!EXAMPLE] Teacher's personal illustration — the kadhi story
> While visiting her nanad's house in Jaipur, a discussion arose about how to make **kadhi** (a dish). Different people claimed different regional versions were correct — Rajasthani style, Marwari style, South Indian style. The teacher's husband remarked: *"Dekh, aaj tumne kadhi se kitni saari cheezein seekhi"* ("See, today you learned so many things just from kadhi"). Even though the topic was simply "kadhi," much was learned from it. **Teaching point:** A person with the Vidyā rekhā will similarly extract learning from anything — rain falling, a bird calling — always taking something good from it. This eagerness/skill of learning is what the Vidyā rekhā represents.
- Formal degrees, if wanted as a separate topic, can also be read — but via the **Jupiter** mount/line, not directly here.

### 4. Risk-Taking Ability
- Meaning: the person **does not fear** any situation.
- If a task/work is started, it will also be **completed** by that same person (not abandoned midway — no laziness in leaving work incomplete).
- **Sporty nature:**
  - Does *not* necessarily mean literally playing sports (indoor/outdoor) — though that is also possible.
  - Means: readiness for any task at short notice; somewhat adventurous; does not like staying idle at home; carries a certain zeal/spirit; can function like a **motivational speaker**, setting an example for others.

### 5. Seriousness / Gravity (Gambhīrtā)
- Comes directly from being satisfied ("santuṣṭ").
- **Not** dependent on having every material comfort — a person can be satisfied even without having house, car, children's excellent education, etc., if the sense of contentment is internal.
- Even in more difficult circumstances (small hut, modest belongings), such a person accepts *"jitna Ishwar ne diya hai, utna sahi hai mere liye"* ("whatever God has given is right for me").
- Shows **maturity and responsibility** on the face; tries their best for family within whatever means/environment they have.

### 6. Does Not Self-Praise; Respects Others Appropriately
- Such people **do not praise themselves**.
- They do **not** discriminate by status when appreciating others — whether the other person is "smaller" or "bigger" than them, they will still give credit/respect where due (e.g., praising a child who does something impressive).
- They **know how and to whom respect should be given**, and they also know **how to accept respect** when given to them.

### 7. Self-Confidence, Discipline, Respectable Position, Success
- Self-confidence (ātma-viśvās) — present.
- Discipline — present.
- **Achā pad kī prāpti** (attainment of a good/respectable position) — can be seen from the Sun; not limited to government jobs specifically — must be interpreted contextually (desh-kāl-paristhiti, i.e., time/place/circumstance). "Good position" = a **respectable/honoured position**, in any field.
- **Success (safaltā)** in one's chosen field/chapter of life — with accompanying reputation (nām), status (rutbā), fame (shohrat), money.
- Carries a **passion/drive** to keep progressing — "I've done this much, but I won't stop here; I want to achieve more and set an example in society."

### 8. No Show-Off Tendency
- People with a good (balanced) Sun do **not** show off.
- The "show-off" (dikhāvā) tendency is instead associated with the **over-developed (atidikshit)** Sun (see below).
- Balanced-Sun people: charming personality, generally happy; do not repeatedly seek validation/approval; no fear of losing things; desires are balanced ("paani ki ichha ek level par hogi, isko balance kar paaye") — no excessive craving, and no fear of loss either.

### 9. Creativity
- Sun relates to **creativity** — art, music.
- Such people are enthusiastic about the arts and pursue related work.

## Sun Mount — Competition and Leadership (Normal Condition, continued)

### 10. Healthy Competitive Spirit
- Competition felt by such a person is a **healthy competition**.
- Not jealous of others; does not turn a lost competition into conflict/quarrel.
- **Balanced response to losing:** accepts the loss, learns from it, and returns next time with full preparation.

### 11. Leadership Quality (Healthy form)
- **Q (posed to class):** From the chart's perspective, how would over-development show?
- **A (student):** Sun's qualities all increase; leadership quality present.
- Distinction drawn between two types of leadership:
  1. **Healthy leadership:** "If I need someone to do a task, I first demonstrate it myself, then get others to do it" — leads through cooperation/example.
  2. **Unhealthy/atidikshit leadership:** Treats others like servants, only gives orders. This is the over-developed pattern.
- [!CAUTION] Chart indication for the over-developed pattern: Sun generally appears in a **poor/afflicted condition** in the kuṇḍalī — house placement may even be good (e.g., 11th house/Ekādash bhāv), but some rāśi (sign) influence there is unfavourable — i.e., the planet is **strong yet afflicted** ("mazboot hokar peeḍit honā" — strong but suffering/afflicted).

## Sun Mount — Atidikshit (Over-Developed) Condition: Full List of Traits

> [!WARNING] Overview
> When Sun mount is excessively raised (as if separate flesh has been added), all Sun qualities become **excessive/distorted** versions of the balanced traits above.

1. **Lack of true satisfaction despite success/wealth** — no matter how much money, success, or praise this person accumulates, there remains an inner **lack of satisfaction**; they habitually **complain/crib** ("God didn't give me enough," etc.).
2. **"Snatching" one's success** — if, in addition to the excessive mount, there are negative signs/symbols or a **crooked Sun finger**, the success achieved is described as **"chheen ke apni safalta lena"** (snatched/forced success) — climbing up by stepping on others' shoulders.
   - [!INFO] Definition — **crooked Sun finger**: the finger bends slightly, similar to how a crooked Mercury finger bends. (Teacher references a photo shared in the group showing this.)
   - [!TIP] Practical observation tip: this trait can be seen even in **small children** — e.g., a child who **snatches the ball** during a game (like football) when about to lose. Observing such habits helps identify what the corresponding finger impression would show, and helps learn palmistry faster by connecting behaviour to hand features.
3. **Impatience / urgency** — no satisfaction, no patience; seriousness is absent; extreme urgency: "success is wanted today." Such a person is willing to go to great lengths for immediate success.
4. **Fame sought through self-promotion** — arranges in advance for their own honour/reception before arriving somewhere (contrast with the balanced type, where others spontaneously honour the person).
   - [!NOTE] Nuance: Every person (teacher notes especially "har female ka") may occasionally seek a compliment themselves (e.g., asking "how did the food turn out?") — this mild, occasional self-promotion is acceptable ("itna-utna toh chalega, koi dikkat nahi") as long as it is **not excessive or a constant pattern**.
5. **Education with ego/arrogance** — such a person will be educated (other hand features confirm this, plus a well-raised Sun mount indicates real capability), but **ego attaches to whatever is learned**, leading to showing off.
   - [!EXAMPLE] Case: A retired Dean of an agriculture university. Even after retirement, his conversation constantly referenced his former position ("I was the Dean… if I were still there I'd have gotten that done…"). He imposed a **strict household routine** on himself and family post-retirement (rigid timing demands, anger if not met), making life difficult for his wife (also around 65 years old) despite having household help. His manner of speaking also became **harsh**, driven by ego.
6. **Excessive risk-taking → dictatorial behaviour** — described more accurately as **tānāshāhī** (dictatorial tendency) rather than simple risk-taking.
7. **Sporty tendency turns into jealousy** — competitive spirit turns into **comparison and jealousy** with others; behaviour aimed at raising oneself even at the cost of harming others (whether or not it benefits the self).
8. **Health issues** — heart problems, BP (blood pressure) issues, tendency to fever. Explanation: excess Sun increases "tāp" (heat), which brings anger and jealousy, impacting the body.
9. **Overspending** — such people tend to spend **excessively and without careful thought**.
10. **Creativity — inability to accept feedback or give encouragement**
    - [!EXAMPLE] Contrast in parenting styles:
      - **Balanced-Sun parent:** child brings a painting; parent praises it, and if there is a flaw, demonstrates the improvement themselves in an encouraging way ("look, I did this — does this look right to you now?") — child learns through this positive interaction.
      - **Over-developed-Sun parent:** finds fault first — e.g., child gets 99/100 and parent says "where did the one mark go?"; or dismisses the child's drawing ("in our time we could sketch by eye alone, and even with all today's resources — internet etc. — you still couldn't do better"). Such a parent **cannot give respect/credit**, and if they receive respect/praise themselves, cannot **digest/accept** it gracefully.
11. **Excessive desire (atī-mahatvākāṅkṣā / over-ambition)** — desire increases across the board: for name, fame, everything.
12. **Overconfidence** — leads to disregarding others entirely; visible both in speech and in manner of working.

## Sun Mount — Nimna (Under-Developed / Depressed) Condition: Full List of Traits

> [!WARNING] Overview
> When the Sun mount is weak/flat (Sun afflicted/weakened in the hand), the qualities are the **opposite/deficient** versions of the balanced traits.

1. **Under-confidence.**
2. **Desire for leadership present, but inability to actually lead** ("leadership karne ki chah ho sakti hai par yeh kar na paayein").
3. **Timid nature** — constant fear: "What if I lose? What will others think of me?" Confidence is reduced.
   - Contrast with the over-developed type: the over-developed person thinks "let someone dare to surpass me"; the under-developed person is instead **afraid to even try to move forward**, fearing defame, fearing parents'/friends' reactions ("papa alag se daantenge, mummy alag se kahengi, mai doston ke saamne mooh kaise dikhaunga").
4. **Very introverted.**
5. **Loss of risk-taking ability** — fear of everything, including (as an example) **fear of darkness**.
6. **Cannot accept honour/respect gracefully** — even receiving a compliment, such a person looks down while receiving it; rather than enjoying the moment, immediately begins fearing "what if I'm not praised next time" — so present appreciation cannot be enjoyed; internal satisfaction never fully arises. Root cause: **low self-confidence**.
7. **Tendency toward flattery/sycophancy (chāplūsī)** — especially if other hand indicators are otherwise reasonably fine. Motivation: to get one's own work done — e.g., pursuing someone for a government job or to protect one's job.
   - [!NOTE] Distinction: flattery indicated by **Budh (Mercury)** typically manifests through **speech** ("bolne se"); flattery indicated here (weak Sun) can occur **even without explicit speech/flattering words**.
8. **Poor health** — weak Sun mount is bad for health: stomach problems, low immunity, frequent headaches or **migraine** issues.
   - [!NOTE] Migraine can also be indicated by other mounts/signs, but weak Sun mount is one contributing indicator.
   - Hair may turn **grey/white early**.
9. **Lying over small matters** — due to fear and low self-confidence, such a person may lie even about minor things.
10. **Reduced desire to progress / low learning appetite** — "same pattern chalta aa raha hai," repeating routines without real engagement or curiosity ("yeh yeh karoge, yeh karoge... na koi lag raha" — going through the motions without real interest). Desire to learn something new diminishes.
11. **Reduced creativity** — creative capacity is generally low across situations.

## Sun Mount — Apex Direction (Tilt Toward Adjacent Mounts)

> [!INFO] Two possible tilt directions for the "apex" (peak) of the Sun mount:
> 1. Toward **Saturn** (Shani)
> 2. Toward **Mercury** (Budh)

### Interaction With Finger Length (Sun vs Jupiter Finger)
- If the **Jupiter finger** is longer relative to the **Sun finger** → the person uses **buddhi-vivek** (wisdom/discernment/deliberation) in decisions — does not take impulsive risks.
- If the **Jupiter finger is shorter** → less use of wisdom/deliberation; the person takes **fast, less-considered ("bina soche samjhe") action**.
- This finger-length relationship interacts with the apex-tilt reading below.

### Apex Tilted Toward Saturn

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| Health/confidence | Not favourable for health or confidence |
| Temperament | Somewhat quiet/withdrawn — like the general nature association of a Shani-Sūrya combination |
| Introversion re: praise | Even after receiving something like a prize/award, the person will **not announce it** — will bring it home quietly. Not that this is "wrong," simply that this person does not particularly enjoy excessive praise/attention |
| Pace of work | Slower in all endeavours |
| Risk-taking | Thinks **ten times** before taking any risk — combined with finger-length principle above (e.g., short Sun finger + long Jupiter finger reinforces deliberate, cautious decision-making; tilt toward Saturn further slows risk-taking) |
| Creativity | Tends to **hide/suppress** creative work rather than display it |
| Progress/fame | Slower progress; **mild defame** ("halka phulka defame ka yog") is possible |

> [!CAUTION] Important qualifier on "defame"
> Defame is **not** predicted from the Sun mount **alone**. A specific marking — a **cross sign (✕)** on the Sun mount — is required to predict defame in a particular time period. Mild, everyday reputational bumps ("thoda bahut toh koi na koi badnaam ho hi jata hai") are common to everyone and are **not** what is meant by significant defame here.
> The more accurate reading for a Saturn-tilted Sun apex, absent a cross sign, is: **delay in receiving fame** — i.e., if another person did the identical work with the identical effort, they might receive recognition faster; this person, doing the same work with the same effort, receives comparatively less/slower recognition.

> [!EXAMPLE] Chart cross-reference example
> A student's chart shows Saturn's aspect (dṛṣṭi) clearly falling on the Sun. This should also be visible/reflected in the corresponding finger/hand reading.

### Apex Tilted Toward Mercury

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| Creativity | Creative |
| Speech | Effective, powerful speech ("ojaswī vāṇī") — expressive; skilled at articulating thoughts |
| Personality | **Extrovert** — opens up and shares freely |
| Business inclination | Creativity gets **commercialised** — talent can reach a commercial level, i.e., can be monetised/turned into business |
| Money orientation | Somewhat **money-minded** (Mercury's classic association) |
| Intelligence | Intelligent; enjoys learning new things continuously |
| Behavioural quirk | Behaviour can be somewhat **childlike**, even in adults, when Sun tilts toward Mercury |

## Practical Observation Method — Reading Small Changes Over Time

> [!TIP] Method for tracking personal growth via the hand
> - Regularly observe small changes in your own hand: increased roughness, marks from cuts, changes in skin/nails, etc. — these correlate with corresponding life changes.
> - Starting to learn a new subject, or writing more, will show as **new/emerging lines toward Jupiter and/or Mercury mounts**.
> - The teacher notes her own hand: a **Sun line** that, over time (since writing her first book), **merged into the Mercury line**. Previously two separate lines, they combined into one — illustrating that lines can visibly change over the course of a person's life. Recommendation: **periodically re-examine your own lines over time.**

## Questions & Answers (Consolidated)

> [!FAQ] Q1: Do ministers/leaders have something special or distinct in their hands — some separate "science"?
> **A:** Yes — such individuals typically show strong indicators across the **Sun mount, Sun's lines, Fate Line (Bhāgya rekhā), and Life Line (Jīvan rekhā)** — read together, not from a single line alone.

> [!FAQ] Q2: (Regarding the fingers-between-Sun-and-Saturn line) How many such lines can appear, and what do multiple lines mean?
> **A:** Different hands show different counts — one, two, or three such lines are possible; a single strong line can also appear "doubled" in some hands. The exact number and origin point needs individual hand examination to classify correctly (Sun line vs. Vidyā rekhā).

> [!FAQ] Q3: Is there a separate marking indicating "defame"?
> **A:** Defame specifically (in a defined time period) requires a **cross (✕) sign** on the Sun mount — it is not inferred from the mount's Saturn-tilt or general condition alone. Ordinary/mild reputational setbacks are common to everyone and not the same as a significant "defame yog."

> [!FAQ] Q4: (Arvind ji) Given a 7th-house Sun, have you faced recurring problems with government-related work/officials?
> **A:** No — such work is generally handled by his father, whose Sun placement is good.

> [!FAQ] Q5: (Clarifying question during Arvind ji's reading) If the Sun is placed unfavourably (7th house) in the chart, why does the hand show a strong Sun line?
> **A:** Because of the specific path of that line — it originates from Mars, crosses over, and passes through an area where "enemy lines" are also present; the chart-level affliction and hand-level line strength are both valid and can coexist, explained by this specific line path.

> [!FAQ] Q6 (implicit, during finger/phalange section): What does it mean if the first phalanges of the fingers are very raised/prominent?
> **A:** Indicates strong progress toward spirituality (ādhyātma).

> [!FAQ] Q7: What does a sunken/pinched third phalange across the fingers indicate?
> **A:** Poor material gain (prāpti) despite hard work/effort.
