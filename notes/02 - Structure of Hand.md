# Lecture 2: Foundations — Naming, Mounts, Major Lines, Measuring & Hand Types

> [!INFO] Lecture Scope
> This class covers introductory concepts before line-reading begins: who we do/don't read, the naming of every part of the hand, the mounts (पर्वत), the major lines (introduced only by name and origin, not full interpretation), and how to measure the palm to classify it into one of **four front-palm hand types**.

---

## Preliminary Concepts

> [!INFO] Why We Don't Read a Child's Hand Before Age 12
> We generally do **not read the hand of a child younger than 12 years**, because their lines are not yet formed, and their independent thought process — the ability to think and make their own decisions — only begins developing after that.

> [!INFO] Square Hand at Birth
> **Every person is born with a square *hand*** — note carefully, the teacher specifies **hand**, not **palm** (the distinction between "hand" and "palm" is explained later in the measurement section). The **shape of the hand begins forming only after age 7**. Hand shape can be studied both from the **front palm** and the **back palm**, and each gives a different set of possible shapes. Hand shape is very useful for predicting **nature/personality**, and matters a great deal when predicting **career**.

> [!IMPORTANT] Reading Sequence Principle
> Before any prediction can be made, the **hand's shape must be read first**. Predictions do not begin until shape has been established.

> [!NOTE] Speaker emphasized repeatedly: Once a person's hand *shape* is fixed (after age 7), it **does not change** for life — only the *lines* on the hand keep changing over time.

## Naming the Hand's Parts

### Wrist Lines — मणिबद्ध रेखा (Maṇibaddha Rekhā) / पंकण रेखा (Paṅkaṇa Rekhā)
- Found at the wrist; different people show **one, two, or three** such lines.
- These are also called **rakāsčaṇa/paṅkaṇa rekhā** interchangeably with **maṇibaddha rekhā**.

> [!TIP] How to See Them Clearly
> Do **not** hold the hand perfectly straight when checking these lines. **Tilt the hand about 30 degrees** (not 90) — a faint additional line often becomes visible with this tilt that is not visible on a flat hand. This tilted-hand method should always be used specifically when doing **time predictions** from these lines.

> [!NOTE] Maximum count observed: **up to four** maṇibaddha rekhā lines have been seen in practice; the teacher has not personally seen more than four, nor found more than four documented in books.

### Fingers and Their Names
| Finger (English) | Sanskrit/Hindi name | Ruling planet / palmistry name |
|---|---|---|
| Thumb | अंगूठा | (see thumb notes below) |
| Index finger | तर्जनी | **Jupiter finger** (गुरु की उंगली) |
| Middle finger | मध्यमा | **Saturn finger** (शनि की उंगली) |
| Ring finger | अनामिका | **Sun finger** / **Apollo finger** (सूर्य की उंगली) |
| Little finger | कनिष्ठा | **Mercury finger** (बुध की उंगली) |

> [!INFO] Thumb (अंगूठा)
> The thumb has an entire independent field of study. It reveals information about **property**, **married life**, and the **self** — one's logic, **willpower**, thinking/decision-making capacity, and personality. The thumb alone can reveal a great deal about a person.

### Phalanges (पर्व) and Joint Lines (संधि रेखा)
- The lines crossing each finger, dividing it into segments, are called **संधि रेखा (Sandhi Rekhā / joint lines)**.
- Each finger is generally divided into **three phalanges (पर्व)** — Phalanges in English, **पर्व** in Hindi — via these joint lines: one at the base, and two above it.
- **Exception:** some fingers (most often the middle/Saturn finger) show what looks like a **fourth line** — a faint or sometimes very dark line partway across a phalanx that does **not** cross the full width of the finger (stops about halfway). This is **not counted as a fourth phalanx** — it is read separately when phalanges are taught in detail, but the finger is still considered to have **three** phalanges.
- The topmost joint line (closest to the palm, at the base of the finger) is also called the **family ring** — this alternate name should be remembered alongside "sandhi rekhā."
- If this base line is **absent** or does not fully cross the finger, that finger is treated as having only **two** phalanges for that purpose; the line only "counts" if it fully crosses.
- The **thumb** is read differently: it may show **two or three** phalanges depending on the palmist/book — there is no fixed rule, and different palmists and different books treat this differently. This must be judged with one's own judgment/discretion during practice.

## Mounts (पर्वत / Mounts)
A mount is the raised, "peak-like" fleshy area beneath each finger, named after its associated planet.

| Mount (English) | Hindi name | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Jupiter mount | गुरु पर्वत | Below the Jupiter (index) finger |
| Saturn mount | शनि पर्वत | Below the Saturn (middle) finger |
| Sun mount | सूर्य पर्वत | Below the Sun (ring) finger |
| Mercury mount | बुध पर्वत | Below the Mercury (little) finger, extending across that whole area |
| Lower Mars | (निम्न मंगल) | The area near the thumb, revealed by the crease that forms when the thumb is folded/brought close to the palm — the full open area is loosely called "Venus mount," but the part between the thumb-crease and the true Venus bulge is properly **Lower Mars** |
| Venus mount | शुक्र पर्वत | The most prominently raised area near the base of the thumb |
| Upper Mars | (उच्च मंगल / ऊपरी मंगल) | On the Mercury (little finger) side, below the Mercury mount, above the base of the palm — this area is always counted as Mars regardless of how much it protrudes; in some hands it appears flat/sunken, in others quite raised |
| — (base of palm) | — | The lowest area of the palm; also part of the mount system |
| Rahu's area / Ketu's area | राहु का पर्वत स्थान / केतु | The central space of the palm, between the finger-mounts above and Venus/Moon mounts below, is called Rāhu's mount area. Some palmists call this "**Middle Mars**." Traditional palmistry did not use Rahu/Ketu as much as some modern approaches do. The area specifically between Venus mount and Moon mount is called **Ketu**. |

> [!IMPORTANT] Mounts Don't Have Fixed Boundaries
> A mount's territory is **not** marked off by a fixed drawn line. Like a plant's roots spreading unpredictably from a seed (sometimes even cracking the pot), a mount's raised area can extend beyond its "textbook" boundary or shrink within it. What matters is observing **how full/puffed** a mount is and **which direction it is extending toward** — that is the basis of the mount's prediction, not a fixed geometric boundary.

## Major Lines (रेखाएं)
> [!INFO] Approach for Now
> At this stage, only the **names** and **points of origin** of the major lines are introduced — full interpretation is reserved for dedicated future lectures on lines. There are far more **minor** lines than major ones; major line names and origins should be learned first.

| Line (English) | Hindi name | Origin notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Life line** | जीवन रेखा | Appears to "outline"/border the Venus mount. Its origin point (not its end point) is what matters most for time-prediction — the end point can vary widely between hands. |
| **Fate line** | भाग्य रेखा | Originates from lower in the palm (near the base); may sometimes appear as two parallel lines; may end at different points, even mid-palm. |
| **Mind line** | बुद्धि रेखा | Generally runs toward the **Moon mount**, mid-palm; its origin point is the **same origin point as the life line**. |
| **Heart line** | हृदय रेखा (referred to as "Heart line" in this lecture) | Has its own separate origin point (shown, not detailed here). |
| **Marriage lines** | विवाह रेखाएं | Small lines near the base of the little finger/Mercury mount — despite being physically tiny, they are said to represent one's **entire married life**. |
| **Mercury line** | (referred to as "Mercury rekhā") | **Not present in every hand** — some hands show it very prominently, others not at all. When present, it can originate from several possible points but is always directed **toward the Mercury mount**. |
| **Sun line** | (referred to as "Sun line") | Origin can vary; in most hands only a **short** Sun line segment is visible; in some hands it runs a much longer, near full-length course. Directed toward the Sun/ring finger area. |

> [!EXAMPLE] Live Discussion — Mercury Line
> One student reported having a Mercury line only on the **left hand**, originating at the life line and running up to the **mind line** (not further). The teacher noted that determining exactly where a Mercury line *originates* requires individually inspecting that hand.

## Additional Observation Points

### Knots on Fingers (गांठें)
- Visible from the **back** of the hand at the finger joints.
- Some fingers show these knots very clearly; some hands show no visible knots at all.
- Knots are useful for predicting **mindset** and other traits — a small detail, but a genuinely helpful one.

### Hair on the Hand (रोएं)
- "रोएं" = the small, fine hairs found on the fingers and thumb.
- Naturally more visible on **male hands**; typically not visible on **female hands** (and may also be absent due to waxing/hair removal — check without assuming).
- These small hairs also indicate certain qualities about the person.

### Nails
- Nails must also be examined, as they give **strong health-related predictions**.
- **Important practical note:** since nail extensions/artificial nails are now common, **always first ask the client** whether their nails are natural or artificial, since it is often impossible to tell just by looking.

## Measuring the Palm and Fingers

> [!TIP] Tools Needed
> Keep a **scale (ruler)** or **pencil** handy for these measurements.

### Step 1 — Define "Palm" vs "Hand"
- **Palm** = only the area from the wrist (maṇibaddha rekhā) up to the base of the fingers (up to the last/topmost joint line, i.e., where the fingers begin).
- **Hand** = the entire hand including the fingers. This distinction matters: "square *hand*" (used for the birth-shape concept above) refers to the whole hand including fingers of a newborn/young child; from age 7 onward, we instead classify the "square *palm*" specifically (palm only, excluding fingers), as detailed below.

### Step 2 — Measure Palm Length
1. Locate your **maṇibaddha rekhā** precisely — tilt the hand ~30° as described above to find it exactly.
2. Measure from that point straight up to the **last/topmost sandhi rekhā** (the joint line just before the fingers begin) — this is the palm's **length**.
3. Example given by the teacher measuring her own hand: palm length ≈ **10 cm** (or roughly 10 cm by estimation).

> [!CAUTION] Numbering conventions in books may vary — some books label phalanges as "1, 2, 3" in illustrations differently. Don't get confused by this; refer to the accompanying picture in whichever book you're using.

### Step 3 — Measure Finger Length
- Measure from the same base point (top of the palm) up to the **tip of the middle (Saturn) finger** — **excluding the nail** if nails are long (a common mistake to avoid).
- Example: teacher's own middle-finger length ≈ **8 cm**.

### Step 4 — Measure Palm Width
- Measure the palm's width from side to side (roughly across the mount area).

> [!TIP] The "Paper Strip" Method
> For more precise or **remote/online** readings (e.g., examining a photo of someone's hand), cut long, thin **paper strips** and use them to measure length by placing and marking against the image/screen. Keep a couple of pre-cut strips on hand — this technique becomes especially useful later for **age calculations** on the life/fate lines, where even a small measurement error can create roughly a **10-year discrepancy**. (The teacher noted that some measurement **apps** reportedly exist for this but she has not yet identified specific ones, and asked students to share any they find.)

> [!WARNING] Photograph Angle Matters
> When measuring from a photo, the picture must be taken **straight-on, from directly above** the hand. A photo taken at an angle will make the hand/fingers appear artificially elongated (similar to photography tricks used to make a person look taller), producing inaccurate measurements. Always request or retake photos shot directly from above.

### Step 5 — Determine Palm Shape: Rectangular or Square
Only **two possible palm shapes** exist (a circular palm is not possible):
- **Rectangular palm** — length and width are clearly unequal (opposite sides of the palm area equal each other, but length ≠ width)
- **Square palm** — all four sides are approximately equal (length ≈ width)

> [!TIP] Handling Small or Ambiguous Differences
> If the length/width measurements are very close (e.g., 9 inches vs. 9.5 inches), lean toward classifying it as **square**, using estimation/approximation. A larger, clearer gap between length and width should be classified as **rectangular**. There is no perfectly exact cutoff — some approximation and comparison-by-eye is always needed, and this improves with practice.

## Front Palm Hand Types (4 Types)

> [!INFO] The System
> Once **palm shape** (rectangular or square) and **finger length relative to the palm** (short or long — compared to the palm, not judged in isolation) are both determined, they combine into **four possible front-palm hand types**. (The **back palm** has a separate classification system with **7–8 possible types**, covered later.)

| Palm shape | Finger length (relative to palm) | Hand type |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangular | Short | **Fiery hand** |
| Rectangular | Long | **Watery hand** |
| Square | Short | **Earthy hand** |
| Square | Long | **Airy hand** |

> [!MEMORY] Memory Aid
> Fire and Water types both come from a **rectangular** palm; Earth and Air types both come from a **square** palm. A simple personal mnemonic suggested: "long fingers → Water (rectangular) or Air (square); short fingers → Fire (rectangular) or Earth (square)."

> [!EXAMPLE] Teacher's Own Hand (worked example)
> Palm length ≈ 10 cm, middle finger length ≈ 8 cm → fingers shorter than the palm. Palm shape: rectangular. Combined result: **Fiery hand**.

> [!CAUTION] Hand-Type Quality Match Is Not 100%
> Even once a hand type (e.g., Fiery, Earthy) is determined, its listed personality qualities will typically match the person only about **70%** of the time. Deviations are largely explained by **fingerprints**, which are a separate, highly detailed field of study on their own and will only be covered briefly (two or three classes) within this course, since fingerprints define much of a person's nature and can even indicate career.

## Three Branches of Palmistry
Together, these three studies make up palmistry as a whole:

| Sanskrit/technical term | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Chirognomy** (referred to as "Chironomy" in the lecture audio) | Study of hand/finger **shapes** — roughly 11–12 shape types across front and back palm combined |
| **Chiromancy** | Study of **lines** (major and minor) and **symbols/markings** |
| **Dermatoglyphics** | Study of **fingerprints** |

## 4️⃣ Audience Q&A

> [!FAQ]- Q1 (Preetu jī): "What should be done if a person genuinely cannot tell whether a palm is rectangular or square just by looking?"
> **A:** It must be one or the other — there is no third option. Use the **paper-strip/scale measuring method**: measure both length and width rather than relying on the eye alone. With practice, identification by sight becomes reliable.

> [!FAQ]- Q2: "How do we classify a palm where length and width measurements are very close but not identical (e.g., 9.5 inches vs. 9 inches)?"
> **A:** When the difference is small, classify it as **square** by approximation; a larger, more noticeable difference should be classified as **rectangular**. Exact machine-precision equality is not expected.

> [!FAQ]- Q3 (Draviṇ jī): "Is a rectangle defined by its opposite sides being equal, while a square has all four sides equal?"
> **A:** **Correct** — a square's four sides are all equal to each other; a rectangle has greater length and comparatively narrower width, with only opposite sides being equal.

> [!FAQ]- Q4: "What is रोएं (roen)?"
> **A:** **Roen** refers to the small, fine hairs present on the fingers and on the thumb.

> [!FAQ]- Q5: "Does the Mercury line relate to the brain/mind?"
> **A:** **No** — the **mind line (बुद्धि रेखा)** is the line specifically associated with intellect/the mind; the Mercury line is a distinct line associated with the Mercury mount and is not present in every hand.

> [!SUMMARY] Key Takeaways from This Lecture
> - Hand shape (fixed after age 7) must always be read **before** any prediction is made; lines change over time, but shape does not.
> - Every finger has a planetary name: Jupiter (index), Saturn (middle), Sun/Apollo (ring), Mercury (little); the thumb is studied separately.
> - Fingers are generally divided into **three phalanges** by **sandhi rekhā (joint lines)**; the topmost joint line is also called the **family ring**.
> - Mounts have no fixed boundary — judge by fullness and direction of extension, not a drawn boundary line.
> - Seven major lines were named with their origin points only: life, fate, mind, heart, marriage, Mercury, and Sun lines — full interpretation is for future lectures.
> - Palm shape (square/rectangular) + relative finger length (short/long) yields **4 front-palm hand types**: Fiery, Watery, Earthy, Airy. These match a person's real nature only ~70% of the time; fingerprints account for much of the remaining variation.
> - Palmistry as a whole comprises three branches: **Chirognomy** (shape), **Chiromancy** (lines/symbols), **Dermatoglyphics** (fingerprints).
