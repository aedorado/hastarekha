### 1.1 Śaṅkha (Conch)

> [!INFO] Śaṅkha sign
> - **Very rare** No guarantee that anyone will find it. Even when present, it is often hard to recognise.
> - Shape: As a conch with a **circle on top**; it can also form in other ways.
> - Indicates a **spiritual journey**; seen in the hands of people at a very high spiritual level.
> - Also indicates **sudden money gain**.
> - **Location:** can appear anywhere on the palm; mostly in the **ketu parvata (Ketu Mount)** area, the same area where the fish generally appears.

### 1.2 Flag

> [!INFO] Flag sign
> - **Shape:** like a "F" or "P"; or a line with a flag-type shape attached.
> - **Do not confuse with a square.** A flag is **closed on three sides and open on one side**. A square is closed on all sides. Example given: a line running with a closed box on it = square, not flag. Two lines emerging with one line under them = flag.
> - Can form **on a line** or **on its own**.
> - **Meaning:** famous, name, fame, increases will power.
> - **Location:** mostly on the **sūrya parvata (Sun Mount)** or the **śani parvata (Saturn Mount)**.

### 1.3 Tree

> [!INFO] Tree sign
> - **Location:** at the **very end of a line** (last stretch), whichever line it is.
> - **Shape:** branching like a tree. May be one branch on one side, two on the other, even three, any number.
> - **Meaning:** growth, prosperity, name and fame. Line strength increases.

### 1.4 Temple

> [!INFO] Temple sign
> - Can form in **many shapes**.
> - **No meaning was stated in this class.**

### 1.5 Chain

- Already taught: chain is seen most at the **start of the jīvana rekhā (Life Line)**; a chain there indicates **struggle**.
- A chain can appear in several ways: besides the Life Line chain, a line may run smooth and another line may **wind around it like a chain (jañjīra)**. That also works as a chain.

> [!SUCCESS] Family ring — the only place where chain is good
> - **Family ring** = the **lowest line on the thumb**.
> - A **chain on the family ring is very good**. The more chains, the better.
> - A chain **anywhere else** is **not good**. Only the family ring is the exception.
> - If the family ring has **no chain**, married / family life is **not called very good**. A chain there is "very necessary".
> - The family ring will be taught fully later.

## 4. Life Line + Fate Line Connections (Timing Method)

> [!INFO] Teacher's note on scope
> The Fate Line has its own chapter later. Only the necessary part is given now. There can be many combinations of the Life Line with the Fate Line (a line joining straight, joining as a branch, etc.; any kind of connection).

### 4.1 Method for the duration

1. Wherever the Fate Line and Life Line **join**, draw the connection to join the two points. That stretch is the **duration**.
2. Life Line age runs from childhood at the start to old age.
3. Fate Line age is calculated from **where the Fate Line begins** (not necessarily at the bottom; it need not rise straight for everyone). 
4. Example: a point on the Life Line may show **35–40**; the same junction on the Fate Line may show **25** (and earlier **15–20**).

### 4.2 Reading: family support

> [!EXAMPLE] Reading a Fate Line branch touching the Life Line
> - **Interpretation given:** A branch from the Life Line touching the Fate Line = **family (father's) support** for career and education; the Life Line/Fate Line junction shows **when** the support started. Example: if the connection is at **15–18**, the father's support was greater for career and education then.
> - **Duration:** the support lasts **as long as that line runs**. If the line runs to 25, support lasted until 25.
> - **Reasoning:** The Fate Line relates to **father / family support**.
> - **Contradiction:** Family supports everyone, so how does the line differ? The **manner of support differs**:
>   - one family says "we spend only this much, no more; do this, not that" — support with limits and restrictions;
>   - another says "we are with you; do what you want" — support given with freedom, so **mental satisfaction** also comes.
> - The first may be support within a limited circle; the second gives support openly.

> [!TIP] Answer the question asked (analogy with jyotiṣa)
> - In jyotiṣa, from the 7th house one can see partnership, marriage, travel; but if the question is about travel, one answers travel.
> - Likewise here: if the question is about **career at 15–18**, read the career line, and a branch touching it from the Life Line = family support.
> - The Life Line shows **all events**: career, money, illness, childbirth, etc.
> - **Three things to watch:** (1) where the line started, (2) where it went, (3) where it joined, plus **what the question is**. "Practice will make it come."

> [!EXAMPLE] Outward line near age 35 on Life Line
> - **Observed:** Around age 35 on Life Line / Fate Line, a line goes **outward** from the Fate Line. It also touches the career line.
> - **Interpretation given:** Expansion, **travel**. Because it touches the career line, one should not read it as a career-age matter; it can mean she went out for the sake of **work**.
> - **Reasoning:** "Be careful with our words, as in jyotiṣa" — the same care with wording.

## 10. Method: Fixing Reference Points on the Life Line for Age

> [!TIP] Aging Demo
> 1. Find where the **Life Line starts**.
> 2. Take the **midpoint of the wrist (kalāī)**. The line may go outward, inward, or into the thumb; ignore that. Only find the midpoint of the wrist.
> 3. Join the Life Line start to this wrist midpoint. Assume the full span as **75 years** ("according to the desha kala patra").
> 4. The second reference point is toward the **Mercury finger**.
> 5. First locate **30**: its place is slightly above the midpoint. It is not the midpoint of the whole span.
> 6. **Halving:** treat the full stretch as 75, halve it, halve again repeatedly.
> 7. **Best way to halve:** take a **strip of paper**, mark the stretch on it, and fold in half. 
> 8. This reference method is for the **Life Line only**; other lines will be explained later.
> 9. Then start to look at events.
