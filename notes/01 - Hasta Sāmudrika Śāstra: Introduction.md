## 2. What Palmistry Actually Is

- Popularly, "hastarekhā (palmistry)" sounds like it means reading only the lines on the hand. But that is not the full picture.

> [!IMPORTANT] Lines Come Later
> The lines of the hand will only be studied about **two months into the course**. Before reading lines, many other things must be understood first — nails, skin texture/color, hand hardness/softness, hand temperature, and other outer structural features of the hand.

> [!EXAMPLE] Everyday Analogy
> Just as noticing someone's nails looking yellow, dry, or pale might prompt a question about their health (the way a doctor would ask), the skin and structure of the hand carry similar information — related to body temperature and other physical factors — before any line is even read.

### 2.1 Palmistry as Part of Sāmudrika Śāstra

- Palmistry (**hastarekhā vijñāna / hastarekhā śāstra**) is described as being part of a larger discipline: **sāmudrika śāstra**.
- **Sāmudrika śāstra** is also called **lakṣaṇa vidyā śāstra** — the "science of physical signs/characteristics."

> [!INFO] Definition — Lakṣaṇa Vidyā
> Making predictions by observing the many physical signs (**lakṣaṇ**) across the entire body — e.g., reading the forehead and its lines, moles/marks (**til**) anywhere on the body, the feet, and even the person's gestures/mannerisms and surrounding events. This entire discipline of prediction-through-physical-signs is called **sāmudrika śāstra**.

### 2.2 Classical Textual References

> [!QUOTE] Source Books Mentioned
> The teacher referenced two books as proof/reference material:
> 1. **Bṛhat Saṃhitā** (2nd volume, by Suresh Chandra Mishra) — contains separate sections on male and female physical signs. Male characteristics: page 711 (Chapter with page reference given). Female characteristics: Chapter 69, page 752 — covering feet, eyebrows, ears, hair, and palm lines, among other features.
> 2. **Bhaviṣya Purāṇa** (teacher owns 3 volumes, has read only the first) — Chapter 5 describes auspicious/inauspicious signs of women; Chapter 6 also relevant; further relevant material appears in Chapters 24–28.
> Author/publisher noted for the Bhaviṣya Purāṇa edition referenced: **S.N. Khandelwal**, published by **Chowkhamba Sanskrit Pratishthan**. This edition was also noted to contain useful material on the Sun (relevant to astrology too).

- No single book contains all of sāmudrika śāstra — information is scattered across many texts, including references in the **Garuḍa Purāṇa** and **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa**, with different verses (**śloka**) in different places.
- The teacher will share specific relevant śloka references (as a PDF) and will post recommended palmistry books in the class group, rather than requiring students to buy the Sanskrit reference texts (which are optional/for those interested).

### 2.3 Origin of the Word "Sāmudrika Śāstra"

> [!NOTE] Traditional Account
> This vidyā (knowledge) is said to have been given by **Lord Shiva** to a sage named **Samudra Ṛṣi**. Very little is documented about this sage directly — only scattered references such as "Samudra said this" or "Samudra explained this." Samudra Ṛṣi is credited with **compiling (saṅkalan)** this body of knowledge, which is also called **lakṣaṇa vidyā śāstra**.

## 3. Origins of Palmistry (Historical Note)

> [!CAUTION] No Definitive Proof of Origin
> There is no conclusive evidence of exactly where palmistry originated. Some say **China**, others say **India**.

> [!EXAMPLE] Cheiro — "Father of Palmistry"
> **Cheiro**, a very well-known palmist, is called the **father of palmistry**. It is said he learned this vidyā in India, specifically from people of the **Joshi caste/community** in northern and western India. It's said the Joshi community possessed an old text written on human skin (leather/parchment) in red ink. Cheiro is also said to have performed the sādhanā (spiritual practice) of **Pañcāṅgulī Devī** (the goddess of the five fingers), for which an eight-to-ten-line mantra exists (teacher offered to share it, but noted she has not practiced it herself and cannot vouch for its method, benefits, or risks).
> Cheiro reportedly had such refined skill that he could identify a hand's gender even when only the hand was shown through a curtain (without seeing the person). Teacher's own short Cheiro book title to be shared with the class.

## 4. General Approach to Reading Palms

> [!WARNING] Do Not Be Rigid
> Never treat any single sign as a rigid, guaranteed rule. Two people (even within the same family) can have very similar-looking hands (e.g., both soft-handed) yet have completely different natures and completely different fates/outcomes. Always factor in **deśa, kāla, paristhiti** (place, time, and circumstances) when reading a hand.

- Hand readings will be done for people of any gender or age; readings will mostly be done **online**, via computer screen (photos), rather than in-person — so students should get comfortable reading from screen images.

> [!TIP] Magnification Tool
> Since fine lines can be hard to see, a magnifying reading tool (available on Amazon; often battery/light-equipped) can help — though the teacher personally manages fine with her glasses. Apps for this purpose are also available today.

## 5. Guidelines for Collecting Hand Photos

> [!IMPORTANT] Why This Matters
> The class will be highly **practical**, and hand-photo collection will begin from the very first topics covered — so correct photo-taking technique matters from day one.

> [!TIP] Rules for Taking a Good Hand Photo
> 1. **Let the hand rest first.** Don't photograph a hand right after the person has been rushing around, or right after washing hands, or after rubbing hands together in cold weather (this changes hand color/temperature). Wait about **10–15 minutes** in a resting state before taking the photo — otherwise temporary redness could be misread as a health/blood issue.
> 2. **Good lighting is essential** for a clear photo — natural sunlight works well; avoid dim room lighting, as it will obscure fine lines.
> 3. **Take both hands.**
> 4. Take a photo with **fingers closed together** as well, since finger **length** is measured this way.
> 5. **Don't instruct the subject to "hold your hand perfectly straight,"** as this makes them self-conscious/tense. Let them hold the hand naturally/relaxed — this way, natural **gaps between fingers** are visible (which carry their own minor predictive significance); a deliberately straightened hand hides these gaps.
> 6. Take photos from multiple angles: **palm-side (front)**, **back of the palm**, and from the **side** (side angle is needed to see marriage lines, for example).
> 7. Take the **thumb** separately.
> 8. Take **nails** separately if not sufficiently visible in the back-of-palm shot.

> [!NOTE] Organizing the Photo Collection
> Don't post hand photos directly into the general class group — a separate collection method (a dedicated group, shared folder, PDF, etc.) will be arranged and shared with students.

> [!TIP] Take Your Own Hand Photo Too
> Take a photo of your **own hand** and note the date. As you progress through the course (over 2–3 months), you will start to see your own hand lines gradually change — a good way to observe the concepts being taught in real time.

### 5.1 Enhancing Line Visibility

> [!TIP] Techniques to Make Lines More Visible
> - Applying **ink** (e.g., blue ink) and taking a handprint can bring out lines clearly.
> - Applying **powder** can also improve clarity.
> - Applying a little **moisturizer/cream** helps too — very dry hands often don't show lines clearly; a light moisturizer both improves appearance and photo clarity.

## 7. Destiny Hand vs. Active Hand — The Core Theory

> [!IMPORTANT] This Is the Biggest Foundational Theory of the Lecture
> Once both hands' photos are collected, the next big question is: **which hand do we actually read for prediction?**

### 7.1 The Left Hand — Destiny Hand

> [!INFO] Definition
> The **left hand is always called the destiny hand** — comparable to one's **birth chart (janma patrikā)**, which is fixed at the moment of birth and does not change; its planetary periods (**daśā**) are fixed. One's *karma* and mindset changes act like remedies, and **transits** also have an impact, but the chart itself stays fixed. Similarly, the **left hand is always the destiny hand**, regardless of which hand a person actually uses/works with.

### 7.2 The "Active" Hand — Which Hand Is It?

> [!INFO] Definition
> The **active hand** is whichever hand the person predominantly **eats and writes with**. For about 90% of people, this is the right hand, but it can be the left hand for some.

> [!TIP] Why "Eating and Writing" Specifically?
> - **Eating** is a basic need; a person will naturally use whichever hand feels most comfortable for it — a strong, reliable indicator of the dominant hand. (Other tasks like kneading dough or dusting can be done with either hand out of necessity, so they're less reliable indicators.)
> - **Writing** is deeply connected to **graphology**. When writing, the subconscious thoughts running through the mind get imprinted onto the page — even a single letter is built from many small strokes/dots, each reflecting a passing thought. Over time, these accumulated thoughts shape habits, character, and personality.

> [!EXAMPLE] Graphology and Graphotherapy
> - This is why handwriting analysis is taken seriously even in criminal investigations (e.g., examining a suicide note for hidden clues that even astrology might not reveal).
> - **Graphotherapy** — deliberately changing one's handwriting or signature — is considered one of the most effective remedial techniques, because changing writing reflects and reinforces a changed thought process, which in turn changes the palm lines on the **active hand**.
> - Nearly all healing/hypnotherapy-type courses ultimately teach the same underlying principle: **training the mind**. As the mindset changes, the active hand's lines change accordingly.

> [!CAUTION] Only the Active Hand's Lines Change
> The **destiny hand's** lines do **not** normally change. Only in **very rare cases** — when willpower becomes extraordinarily strong (an unwavering, single-minded resolve, "day and night, waking and sleeping, thinking of nothing else") — can the destiny hand's lines change. Otherwise, only the **active hand's** lines change, typically noticeable every **2–4 months** as one's thought process shifts.

> [!EXAMPLE] Teacher's Personal Example
> The teacher shared that she used to have many cuts/marks on her thumb. According to numerology, she was going through a "1" period (daśā) — and those cuts on her hand disappeared entirely during that time, illustrating how strong a mindset-driven effect can be.

> [!TIP] Remedial Application — Hand Care
> People doing very hard physical labor (e.g., construction work with bricks and stones) tend to have rough, tough hands, reflecting a tougher life. Softer hands correlate with a greater *chance* of comfort/luxury in life (not a 100% guarantee — the actual lines still matter, but it forms a "base platform," similar to how a rāśi/sign forms a base platform in astrology). As a simple **remedy**: keep hands well cared for (manicure, moisturizing/cream especially at night) — this genuinely appears to have some effect. Avoid letting the hands develop knots or repeated cuts; the impact of such marks shows up around meaningful life events, both small and large, and becomes clearer to interpret as one progresses through the course.

### 7.3 How to Determine Active vs. Destiny Hand in Practice

> [!TIP] Standard Case
> If someone clearly eats/writes with one hand, that is the **active hand**; the other is the **destiny hand**.

> [!TIP] Non-Writers / Elderly Clients Who Don't Write
> Some people (e.g., elderly relatives) may not write at all, or only use a computer. In such cases:
> - If they use a computer, treat computer use as equivalent to "writing" for this purpose.
> - If they neither write nor type, observe which hand they **eat** with — that becomes the basis for determining the active hand.

> [!TIP] Ambidextrous Clients (Comfortable With Both Hands)
> If a client is equally comfortable eating and writing with **both hands**, the reader can run a simple test:
> - Ask them to do the **same simple task an odd number of times** (e.g., 5 times) — such as fetching a glass of water — and observe which hand they instinctively extend first each time. A repeated pattern reveals the dominant/active hand.
> - Alternatively, if in person, **toss a ball** toward them unexpectedly and observe which hand instinctively goes up first — that hand is more directly connected to the mind's automatic response, and is treated as the active hand.
> - For **online consultations**, adapt this — have a short conversation, ask the client or their family, or have them perform a small task on camera; the exact method depends on the situation.

> [!NOTE] Special Case
> If someone's left hand does everything (i.e., the right hand is essentially unused), then the **left hand is treated as both destiny and active hand**. Similarly, if someone works entirely with the right hand, the right hand is active and the left remains destiny.

> [!SUMMARY] Reading Practice
> In practice, **both hands are always considered** together for prediction — just as the teacher never ignores either the birth chart or transits in astrology. But it is essential to be mentally clear about **which hand is active and which is destiny** before beginning any reading.

## 8. Does Gender Affect Which Hand Is Read?

> [!FAQ] Is the Active/Destiny Hand Rule the Same for Men and Women?
> **Yes — the same procedure applies to both.**

> [!NOTE] Historical Context
> Traditionally, the **left hand** was always read for **women** (as the "destiny" hand) — because historically, women had little to no role in major life decisions (even small ones), so the hand connected to independent thought/action (their working/active hand) carried less weight in traditional reading practice.
> In modern times, this has changed: women today are consulted even in small decisions, express their own views, and are far less dependent on others — both hands are genuinely "active" for many women now. Because palm lines are directly connected to one's own thoughts, and thought-driven autonomy has increased for women, the teacher recommends treating **active and destiny hand determination the same way for both men and women** — the old convention of "for women, only look at the left hand" is now outdated. **Prediction should draw from the active hand** for stronger results, though the destiny hand is not ignored.

## 9. Two Common Myths About Palmistry

> [!WARNING] Myth 1 — "Don't Read Your Own Hand"
> Just as astrology has a myth that one shouldn't read one's own birth chart, palmistry has a similar myth about not reading one's own hand. **This is incorrect** — in fact, students are encouraged to **start by studying their own hand**. Worrying that observing your own "negative-looking" lines will somehow make your fate worse is unfounded — negativity only takes hold when a person is already weak within themselves; if there's no inner weakness, there's nothing for it to attach to. Reading your own hand is simply a good way to **learn more effectively**.

> [!WARNING] Myth 2 — "Don't Read Palms at Night"
> This myth made sense historically simply because of **poor lighting** (candlelight, etc.) — not enough light to see the lines clearly. With modern lighting, this is no longer a real concern, and can be safely disregarded. (Notably, the class itself is often held in the evening/night.)

## 4️⃣ Audience Q&A

> [!FAQ]- Q1: "If a person's lines (on the active hand) change every 2–3 months, what happens to a prediction that was already given based on the earlier state of the lines?"
> **A:** The idea is that the person themselves is actively trying to change their situation ("we try to change our own [circumstances]") — this is part of the same willpower/graphotherapy theory: as one's thought process and effort shift, the active hand's lines shift too, and predictions naturally evolve along with the changing hand.

> [!FAQ]- Q2: "Does palmistry also apply to marks/lines found on the **feet**, and should those be counted as part of the reading?"
> **A:** Feet are not counted as part of *palmistry* specifically, but they are part of the broader discipline (**sāmudrika śāstra**) — face reading, palm reading, moles (til), feet, and body gestures/mannerisms can all be used for various kinds of prediction, just not within the palmistry-specific reading being taught here.

> [!FAQ]- Q3: "If someone has a very strong **rājayoga** visible in their hand (or chart) but has not achieved a correspondingly high position in life, what could explain this?"
> **A:** There can be many reasons. First, one must confirm the rājayoga is actually forming correctly (to be taught later in the course). A yoga's presence in the palm reflects a **lasting underlying influence**, but its **activation timing** matters — for example, if a rājayoga forms in the "middle palm" area, its effects may only activate in **middle age** (roughly 30–35 years), just as a daśā activates a chart's influence at a specific time. Whatever happened *before* that activation age (often a period of struggle, visible through the lines of that period) must be endured first. Whether a person actually reaches the promised result also depends on factors like their mindset during struggle and the level of family support received — a rājayoga is a "platform"/opportunity, but the person must still be willing to take the necessary path (the analogy given: an excellent meal is laid out on the far bank of a river, but if you refuse to cross the river, the meal will not come to you).

> [!FAQ]- Q4: "Overall, should we understand it this way: a 'worker's son' with a rājayoga can rise somewhat above his starting point, while someone born into an already wealthy family (e.g., an industrialist's son) with the same yoga can 'fly' much further — i.e., that national/social/economic circumstances (**deśa-paristhiti-kāla**) shape how far a yoga's promise can be realized?"
> **A:** Confirmed conceptually — even the "worker's son" *can* rise ("fly") too, but it will require **significantly more effort/hard work**, and the visible struggle in the hand's lines will reflect that greater difficulty compared to someone starting from a more privileged position.

> [!FAQ]- Q5: "Once the course progresses into reading actual lines, will the effect of planetary periods (**daśā**) also be visible in the hand?"
> **A:** Yes — the influence of daśā periods, or of whichever planet is currently exerting the strongest influence on a person, becomes visible through the **activation of specific mounts** on the hand (parts of the palm that appear more "raised/full" versus "sunken," depending on which mount is currently active). Interpreting these as favorable or unfavorable is a more advanced topic covered later, but recognizing *which* mount is active becomes straightforward with practice — even though the terminology (mounts, lines, etc.) is completely new at this stage.

> [!MEMORY] Key Points to Remember
> - Palmistry is one branch of the broader **sāmudrika śāstra** (science of physical signs), which also includes face, feet, moles, and gestures.
> - Line-reading comes much later in the course (~2 months in) — first comes the study of the hand's overall structure (nails, skin, hand type, temperature, etc.).
> - **Left hand = Destiny hand** (fixed, like the birth chart) — always, regardless of gender.
> - **Active hand** = the hand used for eating and writing (usually the same hand) — its lines **can and do change** over time (roughly every 2–4 months) as thought patterns and effort change; the destiny hand's lines almost never change except with extraordinary willpower.
> - Photos should be taken after 10–15 minutes of rest, in good natural light, with fingers both open (naturally, not forced straight) and closed, from front, back, and side, plus separate thumb/nail shots.
> - Two myths to discard: don't fear reading your own hand, and don't avoid reading hands at night (a lighting-era holdover).
