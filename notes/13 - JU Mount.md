# Jupiter Mount (Guru Parvat) — Palmistry Class Notes

---

## Introduction — Three Interlinked Topics

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Prediction Framework
> **Three topics are interlinked** for palmistry prediction:
> 1. **Mounts (parvat)**
> 2. **Lines (rekhāen)**
> 3. **Signs and symbols (chinh)**
>
> Because the result associated with any given mount is, to a large extent, also determined by the signs and symbols found on it.

---

## The Apex Concept — Astrological Analogy

> [!INFO] Definition — The Apex (Center Point of a Mount)
> Just as in a birth chart (kuṇḍalī), a planet sitting in the lagna (ascendant) — e.g., Jupiter, Venus, Rahu, or Mars — is understood to have a stronger influence on the person, or a planet found strong in **ṣaḍbala** is understood to exert its influence more: **the same principle applies in palmistry via the Apex.**
> - We must find the **Apex** (center point) of every mount.
> - The Apex tells us whether that mount's/planet's influence is in **excess ("adhikta")** in the person's nature or not.
> - **Finding the center point (Apex) alone is not sufficient** — we must also examine whether the mount itself is puffed, spongy, or somewhat pressed (dabā huā). These are separate categories that must be checked together.

### Mount Quality — Puffed, Spongy, Pressed
- Some hands show the **Jupiter mount clearly raised/puffed** — easily distinguishable.
- Some hands show it **completely pressed/flat**.
- Some hands show a mount that, when pressed with a finger, **compresses and then slowly returns to its original shape** — like a **sponge**. This is called a **"spongy mount."**
  - [!WARNING] A **spongy mount is not considered good.** The normal condition should be **raised** — like a mattress (gaddā) that springs back immediately when pressed, not slowly like a sponge. **If a part of the hand takes noticeable time to return to shape after being pressed, that is a distinctly poor sign — its results will not be good.**

### Example — Two Mounts Merging (Sun and Mercury)
> [!EXAMPLE] Teacher's Hand — Merged Mounts
> Generally, the **Sun and Mercury mounts** can be found to merge together in the middle of the palm — i.e., the Sun mount shifts from its own designated location and merges into the area near Mercury, which then appears raised, while the Sun's own original area appears pressed/sunken. The teacher observed this same pattern on Dravin ji's hand as well.

> [!NOTE] Which Hand to Read
> Students asked whether both hands should be examined. The teacher clarified: **primarily the active hand is read**, although some influence can also be observed in the other (inactive) hand — e.g., a student noted their inactive hand showed Jupiter slightly shifted toward Saturn with the mount somewhat raised in the middle.

## Locating the Apex — Method

- **Visually**, in good lighting, fine skin-texture lines/ridges (dhāriyāṅ) can often be seen forming a pattern in the Jupiter mount area — typically **three lines converging at a central point**. That central convergence point is the **Apex**.
- [!EXAMPLE] Demonstrated on Arvind ji's hand (with ink applied): several fine lines were shown clearly converging at a specific point, identified as his Apex.

![Arvind ji — Jupiter Mount Apex (Unmarked)](/lecture-notes-images/13/01-arvind-ji-ju-apex.png)

![Arvind ji — Jupiter Mount Apex with Converging Lines Highlighted](/lecture-notes-images/13/01-arvind-ji-ju-apex-marked.png)

- [!NOTE] These same fine ridge lines/patterns also relate to the **five elements (pañch-tattva)** and to **fingerprints (loops etc.)** at the fingertip — but this connection will be covered later, when fingerprints are studied specifically.

### Three Possible Apex-Shift Directions for Jupiter Mount
1. **Toward Saturn** — the Jupiter mount's peak shifts/flattens toward the neighbouring Saturn mount, effectively merging with it. Whichever mount a shifted Apex moves toward, **the shifted mount takes on some of that neighbouring mount's quality.**
2. **Toward the Thumb (Self)** — since there is no finger on that side (i.e., the area beyond Jupiter toward the thumb), the shift is understood as moving "toward the self" — it takes on the **thumb's quality** (associated with self/"I").
3. **Toward the Mind Line (downward)** — if Jupiter shifts downward, this area technically borders **lower Mars and Venus mounts**, but neither of those is used for this reading. Instead, this downward direction is read via the **Mind Line**, because Jupiter (which gives knowledge) shifting this way indicates that one's knowledge is being channelled through and combined with the **use of the mind/intellect**.

> [!SUCCESS] Reliable Indicator — Dominant Planet
> Whichever mount's Apex is found **exactly centred**, that planet's influence is considered the **dominant ("pradhān") influence** on the person's nature. A person may have **several mounts with centred Apexes**, in which case multiple planets exert roughly equal influence.

## Three Parameters for Reading Any Mount's Height

> [!TIP] Mountain Elevation Analogy
> Think of three possible conditions for a "mountain":
> 1. A mountain that is **very tall** → **Ati-vikshit** (over-developed)
> 2. A mountain that is of **normal/moderate** height → **Sāmānya** (normal)
> 3. What should be a mountain but instead appears **flat** → **Nimna / Avikasit** (under-developed / undeveloped) — different names may be used for this same condition.

> [!IMPORTANT] Astrological Parallel
> As previously studied in astrology: a planet placed in a **kendra** (angular house) or **trikoṇa** (trine) is generally very good; placement in the **6th, 8th, or 12th house** is generally poor; and **excessive influence in the 11th house (Ekādash)**, especially combined with a **retrograde (vakrī)** condition, is also considered unfavourable — i.e., **even excess ("ati") can be a bad thing**, not only deficiency. This same "too much is also bad" principle underlies the Ati-vikshit/nimna framework for mounts.

## Jupiter Mount — Sāmānya (Normal) Condition: Qualities, in Teaching Order

> [!INFO] Teaching Order
> Jupiter's normal qualities are covered first, one at a time, to establish the baseline before exploring how these traits shift under over-developed and under-developed conditions.

1. **Gyān (Knowledge).**
2. **Vivek (Discernment/wisdom)** — understanding of right and wrong; how a person handles whatever situation they face. (Vivek will reappear when the Sun mount is studied later as well.)
3. **Giving guidance/teaching others** — Jupiter relates to the roles of **teacher, preacher, and counselor**; this will be revisited further when career topics are covered.
4. **Dhairya (Patience)** — also read via Jupiter. Note: not every knowledgeable/discerning person automatically has patience, but when **knowledge and vivek are both present together, patience tends to follow by default.**
5. **Ādhyātma (Spirituality)** — can be understood at different personal levels; it does **not** require someone to perform elaborate rituals/worship. For example, someone devoted to serving others or serving animals may consider **that** service to be their own form of spirituality ("parama dharma").
6. **Respect for gurus/elders** — also read via Jupiter.

### Detailed Normal-Condition Traits
- With a normal Jupiter mount, a person **has knowledge but no arrogance (ghamand) about it** — they do not repeatedly boast ("I learned it, no one can explain it better than me, everyone else teaches it wrong").
  - [!CAUTION] If someone does display that kind of repeated self-praise and criticism of others despite having knowledge, that indicates Jupiter may in fact be **afflicted (pīḍit)**, and reflects **excess pride/arrogance**.
- **Vivek in teaching others:** a normal-Jupiter person **will** offer guidance to others, but **without belittling them** while doing so. (Contrast: many people teach/explain things while simultaneously putting the other person down — "you probably never studied this," "you don't know this.") The normal type teaches while leaving the other person free/unburdened, without positioning themselves as superior.
  - [!NOTE] Tarot correspondence: this quality corresponds to the **Hierophant** card.
  - [!NOTE] Numerology correspondence: corresponds to a **single 3 with a positive decimal**.
- **Patience** is visible on the face; when being spoken to or explained something, such a person **listens without interrupting**.
- **Spirituality**, in the normal condition: the person follows their own dharma but is **not fanatical** about it — e.g., not rigidly insisting on performing worship at an exact fixed hour regardless of circumstance (such as when unwell). They respect their own path **and** respect others following different religious/worship paths — **an inclusive person who brings everyone along, without excessive display and without belittling anyone.**
- **Respect for elders** is consistently present — respecting anyone, even someone younger, if that younger person has something valuable to teach them.
- **Bhāgyavān (Fortunate)** — Jupiter is the **kāraka (significator) of fortune (bhāgya)**. Such people are generally described as fortunate — though we do **not** call someone fortunate purely from the Jupiter mount alone. The underlying logic: a person who is discerning, knowledgeable, patient, and able to control their own desires tends not to create their own or others' distress, unlike a person whose own distress creates disturbance for their whole family.
- **Health:** generally good, specifically relating to **stomach, ears, and liver** — the body areas Jupiter governs.
- **Growth in life** — expansion and advancement.
- **Udār hṛiday (Generous heart):** always ready to help others, without holding back.
  - Even without financial means, help can be given in other ways — offering food, sitting and talking with someone, calming them down, and building their patience during a difficult time ("it's okay, today is just a hard day, tomorrow will be better").
  - If financially capable, such a person **will** help monetarily too, but **will not repeatedly remind the other person of it** ("remember I gave you that money back then") — no ego attached to the help given.
- **Nyāyapriya (Justice-loving):** takes the side of right and wrong appropriately, but **is not overly rigid** about it.
- [!NOTE] The **Apex's position also modifies these readings further** — the three apex-shift directions (covered later in this session) create additional variation on top of the base mount condition.
- **Food preferences (Foodie):** enjoys sweets; overall enjoys good food and drink, but keeps it **simple and within limits (Sātvik, moderate)**.
- **Responsible toward family**, **mature** — this maturity/seriousness is visible on the face.
- **Māan-Pratishṭhā (Respect/reputation)** naturally increases for such people — they never need to ask others to respect them; people come and give respect on their own.
- Two more qualities: **contentment (santosh)**, and such people tend to be very **joyful (ānandmay)**, satisfied overall with whatever they have in the normal condition.
- A normal-Jupiter person, when teaching sacred/scriptural knowledge, **does not use fear to compel belief** — contrasted with a common modern pattern (e.g., in astrology) where "remedies" are pushed by frightening the client about their chart. **The normal condition does not involve this fear-based approach.**

## Jupiter Mount — Ati-vikshit (Over-Developed) Condition

> [!WARNING] Esoteric Correspondences
> - **Numerology correspondence:** a "triple 3" in **negative** form.
> - **Astrology correspondence:** Jupiter **afflicted (pīḍit)** and **retrograde (vakrī)**.

1. **Ego develops around knowledge** — general ego is already present, but even small matters can trigger it further; the person **may start showing off their knowledge**, sometimes even without fully possessing it (this "showing off despite not truly knowing" trait intensifies further in the nimna/under-developed condition, but can appear here too).
2. **No real vivek (discernment)**; **no patience.** If asked a question outside their area of expertise that they don't know the answer to, such a person **will not simply admit "I don't know this"** — instead, they get **angry**, reacting as if the question itself was inappropriate ("why are you bringing up unrelated questions right now").
3. **Reduced ability to judge right/wrong, or how to react appropriately** to a given situation — this shows a negative impact.
4. **Stubborn (jiddī)**, insists on having their own way.
5. **"Main hi hoon" (I alone am / only I exist) — a sense of pride/self-importance** described by the teacher as a **"Hitler-type" condition.**
6. **Excessive foodie, without limits** — e.g., if invited to a Brahmin feast (Brāhman bhoj), such a person might take far more than appropriate, even taking sweets home in a container — behaviour that would **not** occur in the normal condition.
7. **Health issue — liver enlargement.** Since the condition is both excessive **and** afflicted, this combination produces the health effect.
8. **Craves respect/recognition and displays it outwardly**, actively seeking to be asked or consulted, going in front of people to be seen.
   - [!EXAMPLE] Gurus and politicians surrounding themselves with an entourage, keeping camera crews close by, ensuring their prestige is constantly publicized.
9. **Insists their own knowledge alone is truth** — "what I have said is the only truth; everything else is wrong." This becomes a kind of **imposition** on others.
   - [!EXAMPLE] If a student brings up something from a book that contradicts the teacher, an Ati-vikshit teacher dismisses it outright and demands rigid agreement.
10. **Not calm — shows increased intensity/aggression (ugratā)**, in contrast to the normal condition's calmer disposition.
11. **Never satisfied** — a persistent lack of contentment; **excessive desires**; tends toward the showy/display-oriented type of "guru."
12. **Overconfidence**, displayed in an exaggerated/showy manner.
13. **Ego and pride increase; speech can become incorrect/inappropriate.**
14. **Justice/fairness becomes self-serving:** such a person appears fair when it doesn't involve them personally ("right is right, wrong is wrong" — for the world to see), but **when the matter concerns themselves, "I am right, everyone else is wrong."**

## Jupiter Mount — Nimna (Under-Developed) Condition

1. **Lack of knowledge**; generally **lower confidence** as well.
2. Even if some knowledge is present, **such a person doesn't know how to express it.**
   - [!EXAMPLE] A child sitting at the back of a classroom who understands the material well, but lacks the articulation to communicate it to others.
3. This corresponds to a **"Guru ast" (Jupiter combust)** condition astrologically — Jupiter both afflicted and combust.
4. **Loss of happiness/satisfaction in life** — even if growth is present due to some other good planet, mount, or line, **that growth will not translate into genuine happiness or joy.**
5. **No desire to learn new things** — everything feels boring; "what's even the point, there's nothing worthwhile in life." This reflects a kind of **"nāstiktā" (faithlessness/atheistic-leaning)** influence.
6. **Spiritual comparison across the three conditions:**
   - **Normal:** respects their own path and respects others' different paths equally.
   - **Ati-vikshit:** imposes their own religious practice on others ("this is how it must be done, my religion is the priority").
   - **Nimna:** may become **completely non-believing (nāstik)** — not even believing in themselves — and may go so far as to point a critical finger at others' faith as well.
7. **Can still, paradoxically, claim great knowledge or even claim to be God-like**, despite genuinely lacking the underlying quality.
   - [!EXAMPLE] Case referenced from class: a chart showing **Mars-Rāhu in the 2nd house and Jupiter in the 3rd house** — a young man (~28–30 years old) who began declaring himself to be God.
8. **Books generally describe an afflicted Jupiter as producing a "charitraheen" (characterless) quality** — reflecting the total loss of patience and the sense of right and wrong. The teacher notes such labels must be weighed carefully against the actual hand, not applied automatically.
9. **Laziness**; **significantly increased health issues.**
10. **Badmouthing after leaving a situation** — contrasted with the normal condition:
    - Normal condition: if someone leaves a job (e.g., changes companies), they **do not badmouth** their former workplace.
    - Nimna condition: such a person **does** badmouth the people/place they are leaving.
11. **Craves respect but does not receive it** — no one asks about them or seeks them out, in contrast to how they may feel entitled to that recognition.
12. **Follower-type personality** — tends to always be in a following/subordinate role, going along with whatever others say ("jaisa aap kahoge waisa hum lenge") — associated with a weak/low Jupiter presence.
13. Has **no desire to expand their own knowledge further**, and instead tends to **criticize/badmouth others** over small matters — because they lack that quality (knowledge/growth) within themselves.
   - [!NOTE] The teacher observed this pattern is seen more commonly, in her experience, among women who gossip repeatedly about others as a substitute for lacking that quality themselves — offered as a general observation, not a rule.

### Guru's Knowledge vs. Budh's (Mercury's) Knowledge — A Key Distinction
> [!IMPORTANT] Horizontal vs. Vertical Mastery
> - **Jupiter's (Guru's) knowledge is "horizontal"** — mastery achieved by going **deep into one subject**, extending and expanding that same area repeatedly (mastery, mastery, mastery within one field).
> - **Mercury's (Budh's) knowledge is "vertical"** — learning **one thing, then another, then another** (breadth across many different subjects), rather than deep mastery of a single area.
> - **Normal Jupiter condition:** continuously extending/deepening a single area of knowledge.
> - **Ati-vikshit condition:** learns only a little of something, then **over-publicizes/over-promotes** it disproportionately.
>   - [!EXAMPLE] Creative act occurs under **Chitrā nakṣatra**, but the **dissemination/broadcasting** happens under **Svāti nakṣatra** (wind/air) — like posting on social media "look what I made." The Ati-vikshit type over-promotes what was only lightly learned.

## Q&A — Philosophical Application

> [!FAQ] Anger and Lord Paraśurām's Mount
> **Q (Dravin ji):** In earlier ages (Dvāpara, Tretā yuga), figures like Lord Paraśurām were known to be prone to anger. What might we infer about his Jupiter mount — was it normal?
>
> **A (Teacher):** His Jupiter was very likely **excellent** — his anger, when it occurred, had valid underlying reasons. Other indicators (such as the Sun mount) would need to be checked as well before concluding anything with certainty — no single feature alone should decide such a judgment.
> - Regarding Bhīṣma Pitāmaha as another example: he taught fully and with complete dedication, giving everything he had.
> - Sun mount plays a significant role in these figures and will be studied separately.

> [!TIP] Key Distinguishing Principle
> A genuinely **Ati-vikshit (over-developed)** person does **not** give complete/full knowledge, because they don't actually possess it — they are largely showing off. A person in the **normal** condition, by contrast, gives exactly as much as they genuinely have — and will even **cite the sources/books** they learned from. Developing oneself further can shift a person into this better condition over time.

> [!FAQ] Teacher's Own Guru's Jupiter Mount
> **Q (Dravin ji):** What about your own guru's (teacher's own guru's) Jupiter mount?
>
> **A:** Based on his hand and observed behaviour/patience, assessed as **normal condition**.

> [!FAQ] Ati-vikshit Mounts and Overconfidence
> **Q/Comment (Jivesh ji):** Confirming — Ati-vikshit means more puffed up, and such people tend to be overconfident?
>
> **A:** Confirmed. ## Jupiter Mount — Apex Direction 

### Apex Tilted Toward Saturn
> [!IMPORTANT] Shift Direction Matters
> It matters **which mount's Apex is shifting toward which**: Saturn shifting toward Jupiter is read differently than Jupiter shifting toward Saturn. In this section, the focus is **Jupiter (knowledge) shifting toward Saturn (karma)**.

- **Knowledge gains seriousness (gambhīrtā)**.
- The person becomes somewhat **"khojī" (research-inclined/investigative)** — since Saturn relates to research.
- Such a person should also have **technical knowledge**, since Saturn relates to technique, machinery, hardware, etc. (Although we will also see Mars for it).
- Becomes **hardworking (mehnatī)**; **seriousness shows in their thoughts, not just their knowledge.**
- Tends to **express themselves slowly** — e.g., when teaching or explaining something, does so at an unhurried, deliberate pace, without rushing.
- Applies wisdom/vivek seriously in their karma (work); **follows traditions and does not act against society's norms — i.e., does not break rules.**
- [!CAUTION] **Health impact:** since Jupiter governs certain body parts/health conditions, and Jupiter's mount is now shifting toward Saturn, **Saturn's impact carries over into health** — this creates a **somewhat negative health effect**, to be studied further when the health chapter is covered.

### Apex Tilted Toward the Thumb (Self)
- Thumb = **"Self" / "I."** Knowledge shifting toward the thumb means knowledge becomes associated with **"I"** — this can manifest as **over-confidence.**
- [!NOTE] Amplifying combination: if this shift is combined with a **large thumb**, an **angle greater than 90 degrees**, **strong willpower**, and a **Jupiter finger that is straight (not tilted toward Saturn)**, these traits intensify considerably.
- **Over-confident, with an added controlling nature.**
- Tends to **prove/assert themselves** — "I alone am; there is no one else" (self-importance, since knowledge is directed heavily toward "self").
- **Talkative/extroverted** — in contrast to the Saturn-ward shift (which produces a quieter, slower speaker), this type shares their views unprompted, before being asked.
- **Slightly stubborn, some ego, somewhat dominant.**
- [!SUCCESS] **This apex direction works very well for leadership hands** — described as a genuinely good trait for careers requiring leadership/dominance.

### Apex Tilted Toward the Mind Line
> [!INFO] Heart Line vs. Mind Line Preview
> - The **Heart Line** and **Mind Line** are two separate major lines; heart lines vary in shape (straight, curving, etc.).
> - A **straight Heart Line** generally indicates a **very practical person** (the ending point of the line also matters, to be covered later).
> - If the Jupiter Apex touches the Heart Line, practicality is added to this person's wisdom — but because this is the Heart Line, the person will also listen to their heart.

![Jupiter Apex Touching the Heart Line](/lecture-notes-images/13/03-ju-apex-with-heart-line.png)

- If the Apex touches down toward where the **Mind Line** is located, this means knowledge is being combined with **use of the mind and intellect** — i.e., **practicality gets added to knowledge.**
- **Resulting traits:** ability to conduct **new research**, use of intellect, learning **multiple new subjects**, and the potential to **earn money** from that knowledge.
  - [!NOTE] Possible Saturn overlay: if, when studying career lines later, the career line is found touching this same area, that introduces a **Saturn impact** on top of this — meaning the person expands their knowledge and learns many new things through that Saturn-linked career path as well.
- **Timing significance:** the Jupiter mount area corresponds to the **early part of life**. If a person's Apex is oriented toward the Mind Line **and** the Mind Line itself starts from this same early point, this trait (using intellect to expand knowledge, with added practicality) is present **from childhood** — the approximate corresponding age is around **7–8 years old.** Such a person cannot easily be fooled, applies their intellect to everything, including matters of their own dharma/karma.

## Preview of Symbols on Jupiter Mount (Full Symbol Lesson to Come Later)

> [!NOTE] Symbols Preview
> Signs and symbols will be taught as a dedicated topic later. Two Jupiter-mount-specific symbols are introduced here early because they were directly relevant to hands being reviewed live in this session.

### Ring of Solomon

![Ring of Solomon Formation on Jupiter Mount (Curved Brown Line)](/lecture-notes-images/13/05-ring-of-solomon-on-ju-mount.png)

- **Formation:** a line appearing in the gap area between the Jupiter and Saturn fingers, which may originate from the middle or lower area (or two such lines together), but critically, its **flow must curve toward the thumb** in a **semi-circular** shape — **not** a straight line, and **not** a line curving toward Saturn. A straight line in this area does **not** qualify as a Ring of Solomon.
- [!INFO] Origin of the name: named after a king said to have understood the language of birds/animals — symbolically representing an ability to **"listen to nature."**
  - Compared to **Praśna Śāstra** (horary astrology), where a reader listens to the surrounding events/circumstances at the moment a question is asked, and to **Tarot reading**, where cards likewise reflect what "nature" is communicating.
- **Indications:** an **extraordinary person**; can indicate a **good teacher**, **good intuition**, and **good psychic ability.**

### Teacher's Square
- **Formation:** an **independently formed square** near this same area — critically, **not** a square formed simply by two lines crossing each other (**a crossing does not count; the square must form as its own distinct, independent shape**).
- **Indications:** suggests the person could become a **good teacher or good counselor**, and that they will continue **learning and growing throughout their life.**

### Cross on Jupiter Mount
- **General rule:** a **Cross (✕)** symbol is generally considered an **inauspicious** sign elsewhere on the hand.
- [!IMPORTANT] **Exception — Jupiter mount is the ONLY mount where a Cross is considered a very GOOD sign.** It enhances/increases Jupiter's positive qualities & indicates increase in knowledge.
- [!CAUTION] Important distinction: a cross formed **simply by two lines crossing each other in the normal course of their path** does **not** count — such intersections are common (lines' natural crossing points) and are not true "Cross" symbols. A genuine Cross must be an **independently formed** mark, distinguishable from ordinary line intersections.
- On the female hand specifically, a Cross on the Jupiter mount can also indicate a **good married life** — though this should be cross-checked against **Mercury** as well, since there is a difference between a marriage that is deliberately, carefully maintained in a good state versus one that is simply continuing along on its own without particular effort.

## Questions & Answers (Consolidated)

> [!FAQ] Anger and Lord Paraśurām
> **Q1 (Dravin ji):** Regarding mythological figures known for anger (e.g., Lord Paraśurām) — does anger indicate a poor Jupiter mount?
>
> **A:** Not necessarily — anger with valid underlying reason is compatible with an excellent/normal Jupiter mount. The key distinguishing test is whether the person gives knowledge/teaching **fully and completely** (normal condition) versus only partially while showing off (Ati-vikshit condition). Other mounts (e.g., Sun) should also be checked before concluding.

> [!FAQ] Absence of Half-Moon (Ardhachandra) on Nails
> **Q2 (near end of session):** Regarding a "half-moon" (ardhachandra) mentioned the previous day in the context of nails — what does it mean if a nail lacks this half-moon shape?
>
> **A:** Its absence is **not automatically a bad sign on its own** — other indicators must be checked together. If absent, it can be taken as a general prompt to pay attention to one's health, possibly getting relevant tests done, since it may indicate some underlying bodily deficiency currently in progress. This applies generally to everyone as advice, but its absence can suggest the condition is more likely.
